SHOES OFF

Taking Off Shoes
in Japan: 5 Things to Know

1
Look for the step
At many homes and ryokan, there is a
small step at the entrance (genkan).
Take your shoes off before it.
2
Some places too
Some restaurants, temples and fitting
rooms may also ask you to remove shoes.
Watch for shoe racks or signs.
3
Wear clean socks
You may walk in socks a lot.
Socks without holes are a good idea.
4
Slippers, not on tatami
Slippers may be provided. Take them
off before stepping onto tatami mats.
5
Toilet slippers
Some toilets have their own slippers.
Leave them in the toilet room.
Customs vary. Follow signs and staff. Save this for your trip!
